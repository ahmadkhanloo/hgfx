## Suggested Methods paragraph (English, for author review)

To test compatibility beyond the original synthetic golden fixtures, we used source-defined input and choice sequences from two published behavioral datasets: Suthaharan et al. (2021; DOI 10.1038/s41562-021-01176-8) and Reed et al. (2020; DOI 10.7554/eLife.56345). Pinned public MAT files were checked against source SHA-256 digests; 130 participants each with two 80-trial blocks in the former and 32 with two 80-trial blocks in the latter supplied 324 blocks and 25,920 trials. This is the population available from the specific selected source files and not a reconstruction of the articles' participant-exclusion rules. With the released HGFX 1.0.0 package and physical MATLAB HGF Toolbox 8.2.0, we applied the same preselected three-arm, uncoupled `hgf_ar1_binary_mab` perceptual configuration and a single fixed 17-element native parameter vector across every block. We compared all forward trajectories at the pre-existing M12B numerical tolerance, followed by trial-wise log probability, response prediction, residual and block log likelihood under the frozen `softmax` and `softmax_mu3` observation functions. Observation agreement used the existing M7 criterion (relative tolerance `2e-11`, absolute tolerance `2e-13`). Missing observations were masked in both implementations without deleting trials. No article-specific fitting, optimizer tuning, original-version analysis or inferred clinical association was performed.

## Suggested Results paragraph (English, for author review)

All 324 source-defined blocks passed the frozen MATLAB8.2–HGFX1.0 fixed-parameter perceptual-forward comparison: 2,540,160 paired numeric elements with a maximum absolute discrepancy of 3.1086244689504383×10⁻¹⁵. For the ordinary fixed-temperature multinomial softmax, the same blocks passed all 1,296 quantity comparisons and 78,084 numeric element comparisons, with no out-of-tolerance differences (maximum absolute difference 2.842170943040401×10⁻¹⁴). An independent preregistered comparison using the third-level-volatility-dependent `softmax_mu3` response, more closely aligned with the published studies' described decision family, also passed all 324 blocks and 78,084 elements (maximum absolute difference 4.263256414560601×10⁻¹⁴). Both observation analyses retained 293 response-ignored trials; 48 blocks exhibited Python RuntimeWarning events, whose causes have not yet been established. These results demonstrate numerical agreement for the tested frozen **MATLAB 8.2.0 versus HGFX 1.0.0** forward/response workflow at one shared parameter vector on two independently published input datasets. They do **not** reproduce the studies' historical HGF 5.3.1 participant-specific fits, source-version-dependent response-model settings, optimizer outcomes, parameter or model recovery, or reported clinical hypotheses.

## Suggested Limitations sentence (English, for author review)

The portability tests reuse participant-level task inputs but do not validate the published inferential results: the original studies relied on older software releases and study-specific priors, sequential fitting and decision-model configurations not recovered here, and public HGFX 1.0.0 does not expose complete MAB plus softmax-mu3 fitting through its compatibility workflow. Matched model-component outputs cannot therefore be generalized to equivalent optimized solutions or superiority in accuracy or speed.
